Background

Cambridge Lower Secondary is designed for learners aged 11 to 14 years, serving as a crucial bridge between primary and upper secondary education. This phase builds heavily on the foundations laid in the early years, shifting the focus toward cultivating independent thinking and deeper subject comprehension. Students are encouraged to draw connections between different areas of study, expanding their academic horizons and preparing them for the rigors of future academic challenges.

Options & approach

During these formative early teenage years, we provide a broad and balanced curriculum that allows students to discover their unique strengths and interests.

The program remains flexible, enabling our specialist teachers to adapt the content to local contexts and student needs. We emphasize the development of critical thinking, creativity, and the ability to apply learned knowledge to real-world scenarios, shifting away from rote memorization toward genuine academic inquiry.

Compulsory elements

At the Lower Secondary level, maintaining a balanced core is vital. To ensure students are fully prepared for the demands of their upcoming IGCSEs, certain elements remain compulsory across all student timetables:

  • Advanced Literacy: Complex reading, analytical writing, and effective communication.
  • Mathematical Reasoning: Advanced problem-solving, algebra, and data handling.
  • Scientific Inquiry: Laboratory skills, hypothesis testing, and practical experimentation.

Guided Teaching Hours

As students transition into Lower Secondary, their learning environment becomes more specialized. The timetable is carefully structured to provide ample guided teaching hours for core subjects, led by subject-specific expert teachers, while allowing dedicated time for the arts, humanities, and physical wellbeing. This structured yet supportive environment helps students develop the crucial time management, organization, and independent study habits that will be absolutely essential as they move into their upper secondary exam years.

Assessment opportunities

In addition to this, all students will sit their mock examinations in the first month of Term 2 to assess their suitability to undertake the final examination in Term 3.

Finally, subject teachers will also obtain numerous examples of formative and summative assessments from the student during the year. This may include sampled coursework, examination style questions / essays, presentations etc, which help us identify a student's potential strengths and weaknesses.
